A map of the practice

How I build knowledge systems

I look for patterns and consider what might make things better. My understanding can change as I find new evidence or ask new questions, so the stages are a guide to my thinking rather than steps I follow once. Experience and judgment help me make sense of it all.

Central stages

Investigate

I start with the material and environment that already exist before looking at individual documents and their context. With that, I decide what needs a closer look, and what I find always has the potential to change how I understand the problem itself.

This stage connects to Record-keeping, Systemic Thinking, and Fletcher’s role. Record-keeping preserves observations and source information; Systemic Thinking moves between individual material and its wider environment; Fletcher’s role helps determine what merits closer attention. AI involvement has no direct connection to this stage.

Return to map

Recognize

I use pattern recognition to connect individual observations and see how they connect. Those connections are opportunities to dig into the evidence for confirmation.

AI involvement provides supporting help with finding patterns and articulating possible problems. Record-keeping makes observations and their sources available to consider together. Systemic Thinking connects those observations to wider patterns, while Fletcher’s role tests whether the interpretation is supported.

Return to map

Design

I shape a response around the problem and what could happen if it's not addressed. I distinguish requirements that address meaningful failures from recommendations, which improve the work without being essential to its function.

AI involvement contributes substantially when exploring possible approaches. Systemic Thinking considers how a response may affect other parts of the system. Prepare for New Eyes shapes the material for people encountering it; Governance and risk shape the requirements and their consequences. Fletcher’s role helps determine what response is appropriate.

Return to map

Question

I follow questions that expose what's still unclear or undecided. For example, a missing connection in the material can point to a necessary investigation.

AI involvement contributes substantially through questioning and decision elicitation. Systemic Thinking helps surface assumptions, dependencies, and missing relationships. Governance and risk shape questions about ownership, approval, and consequences. Fletcher’s role helps determine what can be resolved and what needs further investigation.

Return to map

Evaluate

I consider whether the evidence, the explanation, and the response are cohesive. I look for missing dependencies and unsupported claims, and I ask myself if I believe a conclusion, and can defend it with my expertise as well.

AI involvement provides supporting assistance, while its outputs remain subject to professional review. Record-keeping supports periodic review of the work and its evidence. Systemic Thinking checks whether the parts form a coherent whole. Governance and risk bring functional consequences into the assessment; Fletcher’s role remains responsible for what is accepted as defensible.

Return to map

Communicate

I bring the evidence, the problem that it points to, and the proposed response into synthesis. Different people may need different ways into the same work, and I address that directly. I make sure every presentation remains understandable.

AI involvement provides supporting help with language and creating resources. Record-keeping supports the evidence trail. Systemic Thinking preserves the relationship between findings, the wider problem, and the response. Prepare for New Eyes shapes how the work is presented to different audiences; Governance and risk shape how decisions and implications are explained. Fletcher’s role integrates the argument.

Surrounding influences

AI involvement

I use AI to help question, find patterns, shape language, and create resources. How much it contributes changes on a case by case basis, and I remain responsible for deciding what I accept and what I can stand behind.

Its contribution is supporting in Recognize, Evaluate, and Communicate, and substantial in Design and Question. These differences reflect the kinds of assistance involved across the work. It has no direct connection to Investigate.

Return to map

Fletcher’s role

My experience and judgment connect everything on this map. They help me interpret what I find, decide what it means, and take responsibility for the conclusions and recommendations.

This role encompasses all six stages rather than acting as a separate activity or tool. Professional judgment and intellectual ownership integrate the work throughout: Investigate, Recognize, Design, Question, Evaluate, and Communicate.

Return to map

Record-keeping

I keep track of observations and their sources as the work develops. Regular logs and periodic reviews help me see both what I've been doing as well as analyze the larger patterns across that work.

It connects to Investigate, Recognize, Evaluate, and Communicate: recording source information supports investigation; accumulated observations can be considered together; periodic review makes broader patterns visible; and records help preserve the evidence behind the explanation.

Return to map

Systemic Thinking

I move between details and the bigger picture.

It connects to all six stages, taking a different form in each: moving between artifacts and context in Investigate; connecting observations into patterns in Recognize; considering wider effects in Design; surfacing dependencies and gaps in Question; checking coherence in Evaluate; and preserving relationships between findings and responses in Communicate.

Return to map

Prepare for New Eyes

I consider how someone unfamiliar with the material will encounter it. Making guidance easier to approach doesn't weaken the requirements.

It connects only to Design and Communicate. It shapes the material for people without the original context and informs how the work is presented to different audiences.

Return to map

Governance and risk

I consider who needs to own or approve decisions, what risks a requirement addresses, and what could happen if an issue is left unresolved. These questions shape how I design, question, evaluate, and explain a response.

It connects to Design, Question, Evaluate, and Communicate. These considerations shape interventions, clarify decisions, inform assessment of consequences, and help explain decisions and implications. They are not a primary concern in the initial Investigate and Recognize stages.
